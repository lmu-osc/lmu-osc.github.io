# Improving Computational Reproducibility

October 27, 2026

Computational Reproducibility of research findings may be just as important as their replicability. LMU Open Science Center members [Susanne J. Adler](https://www.osc.lmu.de/people/people/susanne-adler.html), [Mario Haim](https://www.osc.lmu.de/people/people/mario-haim.html) , [Frauke Kreuter](https://www.osc.lmu.de/people/people/frauke-kreuter.html), [Felix D. Schönbrodt](https://www.osc.lmu.de/people/people/felix-schoenbrodt.html) and [Stefan Feuerriegel](https://www.osc.lmu.de/people/people/stefan-feuerriegel.html) contributed to an article in Nature Human Behaviour, where they highlight the significance of computational reproducibility as early validation of scientific claims, leading to a minimization of erroneous conclusions in the collective research field, e.g. in meta-analyses.

Eventually, they argue, reproducible workflows should lead to a stronger trust in science. At the same time individual researchers will become more efficient, by being able to retrace every step of their own work.

The authors put forward a number of recommendations for reproducible workflows focusing on the **SEED** method, which includes

- **Scripting** the entire workflow and data analysis in executable code,

- **Encapsulating**, using relative paths and capturing the working environment, while minimizing dependencies of packages,

- **Explaining** the script and the variables it contains to human readers,

- **Depositing** code and data in dedicated research repositories, while anonymizing data and making the data organization easily understandable to others.
