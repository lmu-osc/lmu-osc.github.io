## Overview

**Connecting Science and Society: Effective Strategies for Science Communication and Public Engagement of Psychological Science** is a two-day workshop convened by **Dr. Marie Luise Schreiter** (Max Planck Institute for Biological Cybernetics).

Science communication involves more than translating scientific findings into simpler language. Researchers need to consider what evidence matters, to whom, in which context, and for what purpose, while balancing scientific integrity, accessibility, uncertainty, and impact. This workshop treats science communication as an integral part of the scientific process and explores scientists’ responsibilities when engaging with audiences beyond their discipline.

The workshop considers communication with the general public, patients, journalists, funders, policymakers, practitioners, and other research communities. Trust, transparency, equality, power, representation, and privilege provide an important framework for these discussions.

**The focus is on participants’ own research.** Through evidence-based input, case studies, discussions, and practical, co-creative exercises, participants will identify the central message and broader relevance of their research. They will practice explaining complex ideas without sacrificing accuracy, working with analogies and visuals, and choosing communication and engagement formats that fit their aims and resources. The workshop also examines science communication *with* society, including public engagement, citizen science, participatory and co-creative approaches, and transdisciplinary research.

Across the two days, participants will develop their own science communication or public-engagement project, apply the concepts to their research, and receive feedback from peers and the instructor.

### Preliminary programme

**Day 1 — Thursday, 17 December: Finding your story and your format**

- **09:00–09:45 — Getting connected:** Introductions through participants’ own research visuals, interests, experiences, and expectations.
- **09:45–10:45 — What is science communication — and what isn’t it?** Differentiate information sharing, dialogue, and public engagement.
- **10:45–11:00 — Coffee break**
- **11:00–12:15 — Finding the red thread:** Identify the central question, key message, and broader relevance of your research; practice explaining your findings to people outside your field.
- **12:15–13:15 — Lunch break**
- **13:15–14:15 — Making ideas accessible:** Develop analogies and explore visuals and experiential approaches without losing scientific meaning.
- **14:15–14:30 — Coffee break**
- **14:30–15:45 — The engagement meter:** Compare information sharing, dialogue, participation, and co-creation; consider who can participate and influence the process.
- **15:45–16:45 — Finding your fit:** Choose a communication format suited to your strengths, available resources, and goals.
- **16:45–17:00 — Reflections and outlook:** Identify key insights and select an idea to develop on Day 2.

**Day 2 — Friday, 18 December: Connecting with stakeholders, the public, and the media**

- **09:00–09:15 — Reconnecting and looking ahead:** Revisit Day 1 and prepare for the practical work ahead.
- **09:15–10:15 — Who are we connecting with — and who are we missing?** Map stakeholders, interests, needs, missing perspectives, barriers to participation, and engagement goals.
- **10:15–10:30 — Coffee break**
- **10:30–11:30 — Does science need stories?** Consider narrative, personal experience, evidence, uncertainty, and the limits of simplification.
- **11:30–12:15 — Speaking publicly:** Discuss researchers’ rights and responsibilities, institutional expectations, press-office involvement, and questions of recording, quotation, and publication.
- **12:15–13:15 — Lunch break**
- **13:15–14:15 — Different formats, different demands:** Prepare for talks, panels, podcasts, and interviews; practice concise messages and difficult questions.
- **14:15–14:30 — Coffee break**
- **14:30–15:15 — Connecting with journalists:** Identify and approach journalists, write a short pitch, clarify expectations, and understand deadlines.
- **15:15–16:00 — Putting it into practice:** Develop and test a media pitch, short interview, or public-engagement activity based on your own research.
- **16:00–16:30 — Reach or move?** Define success beyond reach and explore feasible ways to assess understanding, attitudes, participation, or action.
- **16:30–17:00 — Next steps and closing reflections:** Refine your idea and identify a realistic next step and the support needed.

*The programme is preliminary and may be adjusted to participants’ interests. Examples will draw on media collaborations, interactive exhibitions, and public-engagement projects.*

### About the convenor

**Dr. Marie Luise Schreiter** is a psychologist, cognitive neuroscientist, and science communicator at the Max Planck Institute for Biological Cybernetics. She studied Psychology and Cognitive Neuroscience at the University of Sussex and completed her doctorate in Cognitive Neurophysiology at the University Hospital Dresden, combining research and clinical work in child and adolescent psychiatry.

Her work spans psychology, neuroscience, mental health, and emerging technologies. She brings experience in radio, television, print, public talks, and interdisciplinary engagement. Her science communication and knowledge transfer have been recognized by the Science Communication Award of the German Psychological Society (DGPs) and the Werner und Inge Grüter Award for Science Communication and Knowledge Transfer.
