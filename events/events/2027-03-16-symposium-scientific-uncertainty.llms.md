## Overview

One of the fundamental roles of science is to serve as a neutral and independent source of information. To address the challenges and crises of the 21st century, scientific evidence needs to be presented in a way that is accessible and understandable and that allows the general public and policymakers to weigh costs, benefits and uncertainties in evidence-based decision-making. At the same time, scientific findings are often presented using statistical quantities that are inherently difficult to interpret. Communicating scientific uncertainty is even more challenging than communicating scientific evidence.

Open Science is grounded in the principle that scientific uncertainty should not be hidden but transparently acknowledged and communicated. However, paradoxically, an open communication of scientific uncertainty can also be used by bad-faith actors to undermine trust in science. By exposing the sometimes messy and uncertain nature of research, Open Science and meta-research could inadvertently erode public trust by casting doubt on the entire scientific process.

**The symposium explores this paradox at the intersection between Open Science and science communication and discusses how Bayesian approaches can improve the modelling and communication of scientific evidence and uncertainty.**
